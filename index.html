Light that finds bleeds under the skull.

Research

My PhD is about a simple clinical question: can light tell you, quickly and without a CT scanner, whether someone has bleeding between the skull and the brain? I design the hardware, the light-transport models, and the tissue phantoms needed to answer it.

All of my research is dedicated to my younger brother, Alex. He was a victim of gun violence that left him partially paralyzed on the left side of his body. After years of watching him fight to recover, I went back to school for engineering to build devices that could help him live his life more fully. That path led me to brain-computer interfaces. Alex passed away in December 2025. My goal is to help build the next generation of BCIs that restore function to people living with traumatic brain injuries like his, and give them back a sense of normalcy. You can read about Alex's journey from injury to recovery on his blog.

Hematoma detection with diffuse optics

A brush-optode FD-NIRS probe built around a vector network analyzer and silicon photomultipliers, with Monte Carlo, finite-element, and diffusion models of layered tissue that show where diffusion theory breaks down for strongly absorbing blood.

KiCad PCBs · ESP32 firmware · Python · MCX

Wearable FD-NIRS

Validated a miniature wearable FD-NIRS system against known responses (mental arithmetic, Valsalva maneuver), wrote the real-time processing, and raised its sampling rate from 5 to 20 Hz.

Biomedical Optics Express cover, 2026

Tissue-mimicking phantoms

Layered and hemispherical silicone head phantoms with tuned absorption and scattering, including one with real human hair, to test how deep and how reliably a probe can see.

P-4 silicone · TiO₂ · nigrosin · 3D-printed molds
